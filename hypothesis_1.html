Hypothesis 01

The 1D Particle
Race

What happens when t → ∞ ?

I think the 3D or general formulation can only be done when we truly understand why it works fundamentally. So, let's look at two particles that can only move in a 1D way: straight forward or straight backward. They both have their own axis, meaning they both stand on the ground side-by-side.

Imagine the distance between their starting points approaches zero—they are essentially starting from the exact same spot. Now, our goal is simply to see: if both have a 50/50 probability of moving one step forward or backward in 1 unit of time, what will happen when time (t) approaches infinity? Will they stay close together near the starting point? Will they just wobble back and forth forever? Or will they diverge? Let's see what happens...

Seeing this run live... I just have to say wow. I mean, wow. Why is this actually working? Even though the probability of stepping up or down is perfectly 50/50, why is the average distance between them always growing? Looking at our Rate of Change graph, the 'Cumulative Growth' area is stubbornly beating the 'Shrink' area. The average is constantly fighting to diverge.

But looking at the raw distance graph, we see all this violent wobbling. We watch the graph literally crash down and 'kiss the ground' (zero distance) over and over again, yet over the long run, the distance just keeps increasing. Why? This is so odd! Let’s look at what the raw combinatorics actually say. For any given step, these two particles only have 4 possible combinations:

  • A moves Up, B moves Up (Distance stays the same).
  • A moves Down, B moves Down (Distance stays the same).
  • A moves Up, B moves Down (They move further apart).
  • A moves Down, B moves Up (They move closer together).

Look at those odds. There is a 50% probability of doing absolutely nothing. There is a 25% probability of expanding, and a 25% probability of shrinking. Based on this, they shouldn't move apart at all, right?! The probabilities perfectly cancel out. The chance of staying still is massive, and the chance of growing is perfectly neutralized by the chance of shrinking. So what is the invisible hand pushing them apart?
