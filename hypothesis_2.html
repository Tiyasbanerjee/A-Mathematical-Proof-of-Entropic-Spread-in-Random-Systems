The 2D Spatial
Expansion

Into two dimensions!

Understanding a line is neat, but the universe is expansive. Let's look at a collection of particles starting at the exact same point in a 2D grid space. On each time step, each particle has a perfectly equal 25% chance of taking a single step in one of four directions: Up, Down, Left, or Right.

If all steps are completely random and symmetric, do they just cancel out and huddle in the center? Or does the swarm expand outward? Let's run a spatial simulation of 35 particles and track their average radial distance from the origin.

Witnessing this spatial spread, it becomes incredibly clear. The particles do not huddle at the starting block! Even though there is a 50/50 probability of cancellation in both dimensions, the cluster's average radius expands steadily.

Why? Because in 2D space, the volume of possible configurations increases with the square of the distance. As the system moves randomly, it naturally flows towards the states with the highest multiplicity. Entropy isn't a force pulling them out; it's simply the mathematical inevitability of random systems filling up the vast space of available configurations!

Isn't it beautiful to realize that this very math dictates how a drop of ink spreads in a glass of water, or how hot molecules disperse to heat up a cold room? It is the exact same fundamental law. Nature is a mathematician who doodles in random walks!
